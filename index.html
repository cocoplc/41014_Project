UR3 Vision-Guided Pick
in MuJoCo

Finding and grasping a randomly placed ball using a wrist-mounted RGB-D camera, a Kalman filter and visual servoing.

Chloe Sargiss/ Hannah Murphy/ Angus Tedeschi/ Josephine Thu
University of Technology Sydney
View Code Results Evidence
01 — Overview

Sense, estimate, control.

The simulation is meant to show a UR3 robot arm autonomously finding and picking up a ball using only what it can sense. Each run, MuJoCo places a ball at a random position on a table, and the robot starts from a fixed “look” pose so the ball is in view of its wrist-mounted RGB-D camera. The robot uses the camera’s colour image to detect the ball and the depth data to work out its 3D position relative to the robot. A Kalman filter estimates the robot’s joint states from noisy measurements, and state-space control and visual servoing then guide the gripper towards the ball. Once it’s in position, the gripper closes gradually and holds the ball, completing the pick. The goal is to demonstrate a full sense–estimate–control loop, where the robot reacts to what it perceives rather than following a pre-programmed path.

03 — Results

How well does it see?

Vision localisation was evaluated over many random ball positions by comparing the estimated position with the simulator's ground truth.

This graph shows a single pick run where an external torque is deliberately applied to the arm at around 2 seconds to test how the system responds to a disturbance. The top plot shows the Kalman filter's estimate of the shoulder angle and how it closely tracks the true angle throughout the run, including through the disturbance. The true angle also stays within the filter's ±2σ confidence band, which shows that the filter's uncertainty is realistic.

The middle plot shows the estimated shoulder velocity, which the filter must infer because velocity is not measured directly. The uncertainty starts large but converges within a fraction of a second. During the disturbance the estimate lags and underestimates the brief velocity spike, which is an expected limitation when a Kalman filter meets a sudden force the model doesn't account for.

The bottom plot shows the largest tracking error across all six joints. The disturbance causes the biggest error of the run with about 4.7° error, but the controller brings it back below 0.5° within 1.5 seconds. This shows the system recovers well from being knocked. Smaller peaks at around 6 and 8.5 seconds occur as the arm moves to new targets. From around 12 seconds the error rises to about 3° and stays there. This is due to contact and load forces while the gripper holds the ball. Overall, the results show that the state estimation is accurate and the control loop is robust to disturbances.
